### B, Ledger

- Strongest: slide 8. The headline states the inconvenient finding, the table gives 105, 1,626, ×15.5 and 12% in one row, and the source line names the two windows. This is the slide I photograph and the reason I believe him.
- Weakest: slide 3. The stick figures read as a mother and a child, and the dolls are nowhere.

### C, Every dot is one thing

- Strongest: slide 6. Fifteen rings, tools scattered around all of them, one ring with someone inside. Those fourteen empty rings are my forty groups. It is the one picture in all five decks that speaks to a central steward.
- Weakest: slide 3. Two circles labelled "computer" and "dolls" carry no story, and the slide is two thirds empty.
- Cost: distance. No name, title or author until slide 13, and labels such as "105 → 1,626" on slide 9 sit near the 28 px floor. Slide 8 packs 7,073 cells of about 3 px, which will shimmer on a projector. I would admire it and not go and find him.

### D, People in the room

- Weakest: slide 10. A staged empty chair is a stock picture, and the right half of the slide is empty until it exists.

### E, The talk is a commit history

- Strongest: slide 10. Five lines run into a dashed empty circle and leave it dotted. The trade-off needs no sentence. Slide 4 is the clearest picture of the role in any deck.
- Weakest: slide 8. The headline is the volume ("2'183 to 4'890 commits. Factor 2.2") and the caveat shrinks to "note: a commit is activity". The words about research quality are gone from the slide.
