## 3. Per deck

### Deck A (Signal)

- Strongest: slide 1. The number fills the width and "4 FUNDED" is the yellow word, so the eye lands on what was won.
- Weakest: slide 11. "SINGLE POINT OF FAILURE" is the largest yellow type in the deck and the last picture before the close. It argues against the model.
- Also: slide 5 sets PROFESSOR as a thin white strip and STEWARD three times as tall. Slide 3 prints "ME DOLLS, MY SISTER COMPUTER" in the PDF, which states the opposite of the story. Slide 8 gives 8% and 42% with "BEFORE" and "NOW" only, no dates, no counts.
- Costs the room: four minutes of capitals at full volume. A department head hears a campaign.

### Deck B (Ledger)

- Strongest: slide 8. Headline states the finding, every factor has both absolute numbers, the total row is there, the quality sentence is on the slide with a source line.
- Weakest: slide 1. On paper the number is quiet, and 26 empty boxes before 4 orange ones draw a tally of rejections.
- Role slide 4 is the one I accept as drawn: professor and steward are two equal dots, the steward "next to each person".
- Slide 10 shows the 186 dots losing their anchor. That shows the cost soberly, but it stops before the ask.
- Costs the room: the opening. Five figure slides carry 51 to 95 words, so it reads as a report.

### Deck C (Dots)

- Strongest: slide 9. The hardware column towers with "12% agent" above it. The honest point is visible in one second.
- Weakest: slide 6. Fourteen other groups are drawn as rings where the tools did not land. That is a claim about colleagues with no data behind it. I do not sign it.
- Slide 4 draws me as a small hollow ring with one line to the steward, and all 186 lines leave from the steward. My doctoral students do not reach me through him. "186" has no unit on the slide.
- Slide 2 has no title, authors or group. Slide 3 does not say who got which.
- Costs the room: unlabelled marks. On Zenodo, without the voice, half the slides are riddles.

### Deck D (People)

- Strongest: slide 7. The full history since 2021 with both windows bracketed, both totals and the source. The best context for the 42 percent.
- Weakest: slide 10. An empty chair photo is a picture of the steward gone. It reads as a farewell.
- Slide 9 wants a photo of the Gulu team. That needs their written consent and a credit, and there are eight days. Slide 4 wants a student at a laptop, same issue. Slide 4 leaves the professor out entirely and shows 2026 as a drop without saying the year is incomplete.
- Five of thirteen slides are empty slots, and the one real photo is used three times.
- Costs the room: it depends on five photos that do not exist.

### Deck E (Commits)

- Strongest: slide 2. "add: one data steward to the research group" under the key, with title, four authors and the group name. A funder reads it as an instruction.
- Weakest: slide 4. Professor, then steward, then all 186, with "add: one layer between professor and staff". Drawn like this the steward is a gate in front of me.
- Slide 10 is the best trade-off of the five. The headline is "1 person. 20 hours a week.", the failure wording sits small in the commit line, and the lines turn dotted behind the empty node. It argues for more hours.
- Slide 8 cuts the caveat to "a commit is activity". The sentence about research quality is missing from the slide.
- Costs the room: "merge: both halves" and "warn:" mean nothing to a department head.

## 5. What I challenge in the talk itself

- The abstract promises research intelligence, the data management strategy and five ORD-funded projects. No slide shows them. AI is absent from the abstract and takes three beats.
- "4 funded" beside five ORD-funded projects needs reconciling. I sign no placeholder.
- The abstract lists Colin Walder first. The slides list Lars first.
- 42 percent of commits: by how many people? If most are the steward's, "daily practice in our group" does not hold.
- "Biggest jump is hardware" is wrong in the data. Student projects have factor 19.1.
- The trade-off needs its ask, a second position or more hours.
